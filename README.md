# Quality_Education_Crisis
The South African Education System continues to be of inferior quality and shows a great deal of inequality.  As much as Education System in South Africa improved since the apartheid regime, children in rural areas, and townships in public schools are still experiencing challenges that are not conducive to a learning environment.

The scarcity or absence of necessary educational resources negatively impacts the quality of education and opportunities available to students in these schools. These resources encompass various aspects, including but not limited to textbooks and learning materials, adequate classroom facilities, and technology infrastructure, including computers, internet access, and educational software. Professor Lant Pritchett, a specialist in education reform,
listed a few factors:

After a year of school, more than 50% of Grade 1 learners don’t know all the letters in the alphabet and 78% of Grade 4 learners could not read for meaning in any language. The limited or lack of access to resources in disadvantaged schools has severe consequences that hinder students' academic progress, overall development, and prospects. Addressing the problem is crucial for fostering educational equity, empowering students, and breaking the cycle of povertyThe ongoing poor performance in our Basic Education(Foundation Phase Education)  has resulted in a high rate of illiteracy which has produced incompetent poorly skilled professionals and that has affected the South African economy over the years.

By recognizing and taking action to bridge this resource gap, stakeholders can work towards providing an inclusive and enriching educational experience for all students, regardless of their socioeconomic backgrounds this will help in giving South African children the to perform to the best of their ability, explore their potential and be able to compete with the high standard education system globally. Technology should be of the most importance and be incorporated. Every school should have sufficient quality resources and tools to make this initiative a success and there should be AI programs and programs like coding in place funded by the government to educate the youth to be teachers in our schools. This will also decrease the rate of unemployment. In 2024 we should start creating mobile digital libraries for children in remote places like villages to read and create an app that they can access from the basic education system that tracks their performance and keeps them engaged in technological activities. That would allow them to be innovative, explore their creative side and also learn through games and art.
